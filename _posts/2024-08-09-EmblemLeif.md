# **Introduction**
Leif represents the 2nd Game of the Jugdral Duology and the final Fire Emblem Game released on the Super Famicom/SNES: **Thracia 776**. On paper, Leif is an Emblem emphasizing flexibility by wielding multiple weapon types and exploiting the Weapon Triangle beyond Breaking. However, in practice, Leif's performance in battle is an entirely different matter. I'm not going to mince words here. Leif is often called the "Worst" Emblem in the game because his effectiveness in combat leaves A LOT to be desired. <br><br>

However, Leif's viability as an Emblem changes greatly once you realize something. Leif isn't designed for combat like Marth, Celica, and Sigurd. He is an Emblem designed for **Utility** and he provides ample amounts of utility. Once you realize this, Leif quickly becomes one of the best Emblems in the game.

<b>Arms Shield</b> gives you another reason to keep the Weapon Triangle in mind aside from Breaking. That -3 and -5 Damage Reduction can add a decent bit of bulk to your units and improve their survivability. <br><br>

<b>Vantage</b> is definitely Leif's best Sync Skill as just like in any other Fire Emblem game it appears in, this skill is incredibly useful. At base, that 25% or less HP threshold is a big ask, considering that to even trigger it, you need to be one foot in the grave and another hit could kill you. To get the most mileage out of this skill it's best combined with High Critical Rate weapons like Killer Weapons to bank of landing a Crit that will kill the enemy before they can finish you off. <br><br>

However, where Vantage truly shines is when it's combined with another Sync Skill granted by another Emblem. If you've hung around the online communities surrounding Engage, there's one phrase I'm sure you've heard: "<b>Vantage+Wrath Combo</b>". It is no exaggeration that the Vantage+Wrath Combo is one of the most broken builds in Engage and is definitely worth running. Leif sets the groundwork for that build.

<summary> Explanation </summary>

Leif stands out among Emblems because he has 4 Engage Weapons compared to the standard 3 granted by other Emblems. Normally I hold off on discussing the Bond 15 Weapons until the 2nd Half of these profiles but I make an exception for Leif because, unlike other Emblems, Leif's Engage Weapons play a critical role in how his Engage Skill and Engage Attack work. <br><br>

The <b>Killer Axe</b> is a decent Engage Weapon and synergizes well with Leif's Vantage. Leif's Killer Axe is practically identical to a regular Killer Axe you can buy from the Armory save for having 1 less Weight. That 11 Weight might look heavy but remember that to even use Leif's Killer Axe, you have to wear his Ring in the first place and Leif's Ring boosts Build. Even +3 Build at Bond Level 1, you'd be surprised at how little you get weighed down by the Killer Axe. <br><br>

The <b>Master Lance</b> however isn't as good. In fact, it's widely considered the single worst Engage Weapon in the entire game. One look at its stats is all you'll need to see why it's earned that title. Low Might, low Accuracy, and it's incredibly heavy. At 16 Weight, even Leif's passive boost to Build won't be enough to prevent the majority of your army from getting weighed down by this thing. However, the real reason the Master Lance gets such a bad rep is how it interacts with Leif's Engage Skill which we will discuss soon. To rub salt in the wound, if you've played Thracia, you'll realize that the Master Lance has the exact same stats here as it did in its home game, except for having its Might halved. <br><br> 

The <b>Master Bow</b> is only used during Leif's Engage Attack which we'll discuss later. Even if you could use it in normal combat, its stats are terrible. You'll get much more mileage out of a basic Iron Bow. <br><br>

Finally, we have the <b>Light Brand</b>. It's a Magical Weapon, a property that makes it stand out among Bond 15 Weapons. You've read me gush about the Levin Sword before and how it's one of the best weapons in Engage. The Light Brand is a pretty decent side grade to it. It trades some Accuracy for higher Might, Critical Rate, and much lower Weight. That Luck boost can be handy as an insurance policy against enemy Criticals. However, please remember that since the Light Brand is a Magical Weapon, it calculates damage using your Magic (MAG) Stat. 

</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation (AKA: Why Adaptable is Terrible) </summary>

And finally, we come to the main reason Leif is so heavily disliked and sometimes outright hated by Fire Emblem players. His Engage Skill, <b>Adaptable</b>. Now you might noticed in this profile I discussed all of Leif's Engage Weapons, first before his Engage Skill and Attack. I switched things up here because you need the context his Engages Weapons provide to understand why Leif's Engage Skill and Attack are not well regarded.<br><br>

On paper, Adaptable sounds like an alright Sync Skill as it synergizes with Vantage and prioritizes the Weapons Triangle so Arms Shield will activate and reduce the damage you take. The fact that almost every Unit Type in the game gets a Bonus from Adaptable is just the cherry on top. In practice, however, there are a lot of factors that hold this skill back.<br><br>

You might have noticed that in this skill's description, I put "best" in bolded quotes because it's the crux of this issue. Leif's AI has a very strange definition of "best" and a YouTuber summed it up perfectly.<br><br>

<b>"... Leif's idea of "best" weapon is suboptimal at best and outright suicidal at worst".</b> <br><br>

The in-game description doesn't tell you this and nothing in the game hints at this, but Adaptable determines the "best" weapon using the following criteria in this order. <br>

<ol>
  <li>The Weapon allows the User to counterattack.</li>
  <li>The Weapon has Weapon Triangle Advantage or at least, does not have a disadvantage.</li>
  <li>The Weapon deals Effectiveness Damage.</li>
  <li>The Weapon has the highest combined Might, Hit, Crit, and Dodge.</li>
  <li>The Weapon has the highest position in the user's Inventory. </li>
</ol> 

As you can see, Adaptable prioritizes counterattacking and avoiding Break via Weapon Triangle Disadvantage. Say, for example, you have a Sword equipped and a Lance Enemy attacks you. Adaptable will have you switch to an Axe (either one of your own or Leif's Killer Axe) to avoid Break and activate Arms Shield. Sounds good so far right, so what's the issue with this Engage Skill? <br><br>

Well, if you're eagle-eyed, you may have noticed that Adaptable considers many properties of your weapon except one: <b>WEIGHT</b>. This is the biggest problem with Adaptable as Leif's AI doesn't pay attention to the weight of the weapons in your inventory. As I've pointed out, Leif's Engage Weapons are rather heavy, especially the Master Lance. <br><br>

Say, for example, you are Engaged with Leif and have an Iron Axe in your inventory alongside Leif's Killer Axe and Master Lance. A Sword Enemy attacks you and Adaptable triggers with 2 options. Keep in mind that most Sword Units (Swordfighters, Heroes, Swordmasters, etc.) have high Speed stats.<br><br>

<table>
  <tr>
    <th>Option 1</th>
    <th>Option 2</th>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td>Keep the Iron Axe Equipped. You get broken but you only take one hit for <b>13 Damage</b>. </td>
    <td> Switch to the Master Lance. You aren't broken when hit and Arms Shield+ activates. However, the Master Lance weighs you down so much that the enemy now has 5 more SPD than you. You get attacked <b>TWICE</b> for a total of <b>16 Damage</b>. </td>
  </tr>

</table>

Leif will almost always pick Option 2 because, as stated before, Leif's AI prioritizes counterattacking and avoiding Break. In most scenarios, The Master Lance will always meet the first condition since it's a 1-2 Range Weapon. Leif's AI doesn't care if the Master Lance weighs you down so much that you get doubled. This is further compounded if an enemy attacks you at range with Magic, Bows, or Daggers. If you have no other Ranged Options to counter them (Javelin, Hand Axe, Magic, or you haven't unlocked the Light Brand yet), instead of taking the hit, Leif will force the Master Lance into your hands to counterattack. In this scenario, Arms Shield won't trigger because Lances only have Weapon Triangle Advantage over Swords. <br><br>

I am not exaggerating when I say there are numerous horror stories of Adaptable literally getting units <b>KILLED</b> because Leif forced the Master Lance onto them. I have experienced this firsthand. <br><br>

After all of that, you might be thinking, "If this Skill is so bad, then I'll just have only one weapon in my inventory so Adaptable can't trigger.". That won't work because Adaptable is an Engage Skill meaning you have to be in Leif's Engage Form to use it. Once you're in an Emblem's Engage Form, you automatically have access to at least one of their Engage Weapons, meaning you will always have multiple weapons to trigger Adaptable.

</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

If you thought things couldn't get worse after the train wreck that is Adaptable, you'd be right. <b>Quadruple Hit (QH)</b> is a tier above Adaptable in how terrible it is. When you use QH, you'll notice that it is doing laughably pathetic damage. Unlike other Engage Attacks like Marth's Lodestar Rush and Sigurd's Override which let you use other weapons aside from their respective Engage Weapons such as Smash Weapons, Leif's QH ALWAYS forces you to use his four Engage Weapons, each of which have their fair share of problems. <br><br>

The Killer Axe is fine with its 11 Might but the Master Lance and Master Bow have a pathetic Might of 7 and 6 respectively. The Light Brand, despite its impressive 15 Might is a Magical Weapon, meaning its damage scales off your Magic Stat. All of these factors make it so that unless Leif's bearer has good Strength and Magic Stats, QH isn't going to hit harder than a wet noodle. <br><br>

The Class Bonuses don't help much either as they are like putting a band-aid on a gaping wound. Dragons get the best deal with a flat damage increase but Alear isn't exactly a powerhouse to make this attack reliable. Qi Adepts break the foe regardless of whether or not they do damage. This can be nice but if your enemy is using a Sword, Axe, or Lance, there's a high chance they'd been broken anyway and Qi Adepts break all 2-Range Weapon Types (Knives, Bows, Tomes) with their basic attacks. You could use this to break an Armor Unit. Lastly, the Covert Bonus may seem strange but it's a nice reference to the "Leif's Blade" weapon in Fire Emblem: Awakening which had a similar mechanic. <br><br>

All in all, QH isn't an Engage Attack that will wipe foes off the map. But, if you want to risk using it, there are ways to patch up its damage. <br>

<ul>

<li>
<b>Divinely Inspiring</b>: Alear's Personal Skill which grants a flat +3 Damage to adjacent allies goes a fair bit in pumping up QH's damage. Since it's a flat Damage Bonus applied after regular Damage Calculations, there's a higher chance of breaking the foe if Leif's bearer isn't a Qi Adept
</li>

<li>
<b>Momentum</b>: All 4 Hits of QH gain the damage boost from Momentum since the game counts them all as a single hit of an attack. 
</li>

<li>
<b>Rush Effect</b>: Like all other Engage Attacks that strike multiple times, all 4 Hits of QH are capable of triggering skills like Ignis, Luna, Sandstorm, etc. for increased damage. 
</li>

<li>
<b>Sword Power</b>: This Inheritable Skill boosts damages from Swords and is obtainable from an Emblem you obtain in the same Chapter as Leif himself. For some reason (most likely an oversight), the damage boost from Sword Power affects all 4 hits of QH when logically, it should only affect the Light Brand hit. However, don't look a Gift Horse in the Mouth and appreciate this little blessing. 
</li>

</ul> 

While Leif's kit has been rather lackluster so, I can assure you there's nowhere to go but up from here.


</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation (AKA: Why Leif is also AMAZING!!!) </summary>

Remember how I stated that Leif is an Emblem best suited for Utility? Well, look no further than Leif's Skill Inheritance. I make no exaggeration when I say that Leif has some of, if not THE best Skill Inheritance in the game bar none and it's not even a contest. <br>

<ul>
<li>
The least impressive of Leif's Inheritance is his Weapon Skill <b>Knife Precision</b>. These can be a good way to make your Knife users (especially Thieves) more accurate and better Dodge Tanks. </li><br>

<li>
<b>Arms Shield</b> and its upgraded form can add a fair bit of bulk to your units and they aren't too expensive to inherit. Vantage is also rather cheap and that 500 SP is more than worth it, not only for regular use but also for setting the infamous Vantage+Wrath Combo. And this all comes without the baggage of wearing Leif's Ring. </li><br>

<li>
Leif's Stat Skill, the <b>Build+</b> Skills are incredibly useful for pretty much the majority of your army, regardless of class. Having a high Build Stat decreases the chances of a unit being significantly weighed down by their weapons and a lot of the better weapons in Engage lean towards the heavy side when it comes to their Weight. The cherry on top is these skills are quite affordable for the sheer value they provide. However, everything we have discussed so far in this section cannot hold a candle to Leif's main claim to fame: His <b>Weapon Proficiencies</b>. </li><br>

<li>
No, you are not hallucinating. Leif gives <b>SIX (6) Weapon Proficiencies</b> for Reclassing. To put this into perspective, Engage has a total of Eight (8) Weapon Proficiencies, and most Emblems on average, only grant 2. If you're wondering why Leif has so many Proficiencies under his belt, it's a reference to his promoted Master Knight Class in Genealogy of The Holy War where he could use 9 out of the 10 Weapon Types present in that game. </li>

</ul>

I cannot stress enough how much of a difference Leif's mere presence makes for Reclassing. Leif opens up the potential for near-infinite Reclassing options for your entire army. The only Proficiencies Leif doesn't provide are Tomes and Arts. Tome Proficiency isn't a problem because, by the time you have Leif, you also have Celica and another magic-focused Emblem, both of whom provide Tome Proficiency. By the time you have Leif, you have access to almost EVERY single Class in the game, barring Qi Adept Classes. This alone more than makes up for Leif's terrible combat as no other Emblem in the game comes even close to this amount of Reclassing potential. 

</details>

# **Character Builds**



Despite saying that Leif is an Emblem designed more for utility than combat, some builds can patch some of Leif's glaring weaknesses.

**Rule of Thumb**: <u>Never Engage with Leif</u>. Leif's Engage Form isn't worth it because Adaptable can get you killed and QH isn't all that great of an Engage Attack unless you have crazy high STR and MAG to make it hit harder than a wet noodle. 

<details>
<summary> The Silent Treatment </summary>

The main issue with Leif's Engage Form is the Master Lance's anti-synergy with Adaptable. However, this is a way around it and the answer is much simplier than you would expect. If you get a unit to Bond Level 9 with Leif and NEVER initiate his Rank B Bond Conversation, you will never unlock Bond Level 10 and the Master Lance. Leif can't force the Master Lance on you if it isn't unlocked. This build is more practical than you'd think as not only does Leif's passive stat bonuses max out at Bond 9 but everything you'd want from Leif's Skill Inheritance can be found below Bond 10. 

</details>


<details>
<summary> Vantage+Wrath Remix Edition </summary>

If you've gone all in and completed Leif's Paralogue for his higher Bond Levels, I'd recommend this build. This involves inheriting the Wrath Skill from a later Emblem onto Leif's main user. Since Leif's user will always have Vantage as a Sync Skill, they can run this build as well for Critical Hit shenanigans. While you could run this build with Leif's Killer Axe, that runs the risk of Adaptable screwing you over because at Bond 10 and above, you will access to the Master Lance and I've already explained why that is a bad thing. I recommend doing this build with a Killer Weapon with an Engrave that specifically boosts Critical Rate. 

</details>



# **Final Thoughts** <br>
Leif is a strange case as his viability (and placement on Tier Lists) depends on whether you're the type of Player to prioritize Combat or Utility.

On the battlefield, Leif is more of a liability than an asset who is more likely to do the enemy's job of killing your units. The biggest issue with Leif's combat is his Engage Form. Adaptable has a high chance of screwing you over and getting someone killed via the Master Lance. Quadruple Hit takes too much effort and resources to make decent. 

Off the battlefield, however, is where Leif truly shines. His Skill Inheritance is a game-changer for your entire army as there is something in there that everyone can appreciate. I am not exaggerating when I say that Leif has some of the best sheer utility among all the Emblems in the game (both Base and DLC). Whether it be Vantage, Arms Shield, Build+, or his 6 Weapon Proficiencies, Leif should not be slept on.
