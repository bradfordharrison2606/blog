# **Introduction**
Marth represents the Archanea Games, specifically the 1st Game (Shadow Dragon and Blade of Light), the 3rd Game (Mystery of the Emblem), and their respective remakes (Shadow Dragon and New Mystery of the Emblem). As an Emblem, Marth specializes in Dodge-Tanking, dealing with Armored and Cavalry Foes, and attacking multiple times. He is designed to turn his wearer into an overall combat unit. This makes him very simple to use, befitting not only the first Emblem you receive in the game but also the Lord who started it all.

<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Perceptive</b> is designed not only to make Marth's wearer a better dodge tank but also subtly encourage the Player to attack dangerous enemies on the Player Phase as that +15 Avoid goes a long way in ensuring you don't take a hit. Engage is a very Player Phase-focused game that encourages the Player to be aggressive with the enemy. <br><br>

<b>Break Defenses</b> is Marth's most powerful Sync Skill as it encourages the player to familiarize themselves with the Weapon Triangle and Break System of Engage. That extra attack, even if it's only at half damage, can often be enough to kill an enemy in a single round of combat. Like Perceptive, this only works during the Player Phase, encouraging you to be aggressive and take the fight to the enemy. <br><br>

<b>Unyielding</b> is there as a security net/insurance policy. Marth may make his wielder better dodge-tanks, but eventually, they will take a hit and in the early game where your Units are rather fragile taking a hit is going to hurt. Thus Unyielding comes in handy to nurse them back to health. <br>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>
<b>Divine Speed</b> is an upgrade of Break Defenses, except instead of being Player-Phase only, it is <b>ALWAYS active</b>. This means that while you're Engaged with Marth, you'll always have an additional attack regardless of the current phase. However, the true beauty of Divine Speed is that it <b>STACKS with Break Defenses</b>. <br><br>

What this means is that if you're in Marth's Engage Form and break your opponent, that's at least 3 Attacks you're getting in. 4 Attacks if you're doubling them, and 8 Attacks if you're using a Brave Weapon. As you'd imagine, Divine Speed on a unit with high Speed really lets them rack up the damage for reliable 1-Round kills, extra so if they're wielding a Brave Weapon. Keep in mind that Divine Speed doesn't work with Weapons that cannot double attack such as Smash Weapons.<br><br>

As for the Class Bonuses, Dragons healing themselves on the extra hit is nice but Covert Units definitely get the best deal as the extra hit from Divine Speed inflicts Poison if it lands. In Engage, Poison is a static debuff that adds "True Damage" to your attacks, +1 Damage for each stack of Poison up to +3 Damage. This really lets not only Marth's wearer amp up their damage but everyone else in your army gets to join in on the fun. However, this Poison effect synergizes dangerously well with Marth's Engage Attack.


</details>

## Engage Attack

| ![Lodestar Rush Pic]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/marth/lodestarrush.webp) <br> **Lodestar Rush** | Use to launch 7 consecutive Sword attacks at 30% damage. Adjacent foe only. |

        <tr>
            <td>Mystical </td> <!-- Unit Type  -->
            <td>Damage based on Magic (MAG)</td>
        </tr>
    </table>
</div>


<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

As the first Engage Attack you have access to, <b>Lodestar Rush</b> is "Simple but Practical" like the rest of Marth's kit. In the early game, Lodestar Rush is basically a "<b>Delete One Enemy" Button</b>. It is best used on dangerous enemies that need to die right away or on bosses to either kill them or soften them up. As long as you aren't targetting something too bulky like an Armored Unit or Marth's user has low Strength, there aren't many enemies who are going to survive 7 attacks, even if they're only at 30% Damage. However do remember that Lodestar Rush can only be used with Swords, either one of your own or with one of Marth's Engage Weapons. <br><br>

The Class Bonuses are all-around useful. Dragons getting 9 Hits is especially great as it makes up for Alear not having the best raw Strength Stat, especially in the Early Game. Backup Units only getting 8 Hits might seem like the short end of the stick compared to Dragons but all playable Backup Classes are Physical Classes with decent Strength Stats, meaning they won't mind only having 8 hits. Mystical Units basing the damage on their MAG stat is incredibly useful for units with high Magic Stats. <br><br>

In practical use cases, if you want to maximize your Lodestar Rush Damage, I suggest using it with either a forged <b>Smash Weapon like a Steel or Silver Blade</b> for the high base Might or with a <b>Forged Levin Sword</b> so the damage is calculated using your Magic Stat and hits the foe's RES stat. I lean more towards using Smash Weapons with Lodestar Rush as enemies cannot counterattack when you use Engage Attacks which negates the main drawback of Smash Weapons. <br><br>

Remember how Divine Speed's Covert Bonus inflicts Poison on the target. This Poison synergizes incredibly well with Lodestar Rush as if you're using it on an opponent with 3 Stacks of Poison, that's +3 Damage to EACH hit of Lodestar Rush. In the Late Game Lodestar Rush's odds of killing an enemy decrease noticeably. However, it still has use for softening up especially tough enemies like Bosses. Remember, that enemies can't counterattack when you use Engage Attacks, so it's basically free damage since the enemy can't hit you back. <br><br>

One last thing that the game doesn't tell you is that Lodestar Rush and Engage Attacks like it have a chance to trigger skills like Sol, Luna, Ignis, Sandstorm, etc on EACH hit of the Engage Attack. So if the RNG is good to you, you could potentially activate any of these skills and boost your damage. In future profiles, I will refer to this as "<b>Rush Effect</b>" for convenience.


</details>

<summary> Explanation </summary>

Marth's Skill Inheritance is a mix of cheap and expensive. <br><br>

<b>Break Defenses</b> is the main prize here but fittingly for what it does, it is very expensive, even by Late Game standards. In the Early Game, you're affording this unless you make liberal use of the well and skirmishes. <br><br>

<b>Perceptive and Unyielding</b> are pretty dirt cheap for the effects they give you. Personally, between these two I usually go with Perceptive as that Avoid bonus is really handy in the Early Game as most of your units aren't all that bulky, and the extra avoid could save them from taking a hit. <br><br>

Every Emblem in Engage has Inheritable Skills that grant bonuses to either a unit's raw Stats (<b>Stat Skills</b>) or when a unit uses a specific weapon type (<b>Weapon Skill</b>). <br><br>

For Marth, his Stat Skill is the <b>Avoid+</b> line of Skills. I find these skills to be useful as again, you can never have too much Avoid. However, they are expensive, especially at higher levels. The highest I go with these skills is Avoid+15. <br><br>

Marth's Weapon Skill is <b>Sword Agility</b>. At first glance, it may seem counter-intuitive. Why would you sacrifice Critical Chance for more Avoid? Well, 2 Things. 1) Notice how the Avoid bonus increases with higher levels of the skill but the Crit penalty remains the same. 2) Engage has many tools that can boost your Critical Rate to near comical levels (Engraves, Certain Inheritable Skills, etc) so most of the time that -10 Crit won't be much of an issue. Even if you aren't running a Crit Build, the extra Avoid is always helpful, especially since Sword units in Engage tend to be better Dodge Tanks. <br><br>

Lastly, Marth's only Weapon Proficiency is <b>Sword Proficiency</b> which may seem lacklustre but there are many good classes in Engage that open up once a Unit has Sword Proficiency. The standout examples are Mage Knight and Griffin Knight for Levin Sword usage and Hero for better Chain Attack Utility. The main downside is that you get this from Marth at Bond 8 when there is another Early Game Emblem that teaches Sword Proficiency a bit earlier.

</details>

# **Character Builds**
From personal experience, the way I run Marth depends on whether I’m in the Early or Late Game. In the Early Game, I prefer to staple Marth to Alear so they can get to Bond 10 as fast as possible for Mercurius and its EXP doubling. Engage is a game where you want to promote a unit as soon as they reach Level 10 and Alear is no exception as Divine Dragon is a good promoted class. However, if there’s an early joining Unit I want to take to the end, they usually get Marth so they can start snowballing. By the Late Game, you have so many options for builds involving him. I default to one of the following three.



<details>
<summary> Basic Sword Lord</summary>
Marth shines best on Physical Units, preferably a Backup Unit that specializes in Swords (<b>Successeur, Hero, Swordmaster, etc.</b>) and has enough Speed to reliably double-attack enemies. Backup Units usually have enough of a raw Strength stat to make up for the Rapier's lackluster Might against Armored Foes. <br><br>

Lastly, most promoted Sword Units have a Sword Rank of B at the lowest. This allows them to wield a Steel Blade to pump up the raw damage of Lodestar Rush's 8 Hits.


</details>




<details>
<summary> Marth, Prince of Thieves </summary>
 
Recall the Covert Unit Bonus of Divine Speed which inflicts Poison on enemies. This build works especially well on Thieves as their Knives always inflict poison if they do damage which adds more damage to Lodestar Rush. Since Thieves are Cover Units, they get Avoid bonuses from terrain doubled which stacks well with Marth's Perceptive and Avoid+ Skill to make them almost impossible to hit. <br><br>

The downside of this Build is there are no playable Covert Classes in Engage who can use Swords. So, when using Lodestar Rush, you'll have to use Marth's Engage Weapons which can make your damage suffer a bit.

</details>


<details>
<summary> Who needs the Rapier when you have a Levin Sword? </summary>

The <b>Levin Sword</b> is one of the best weapons in Engage and when combined with Marth's Break Defenses and Divine Speed, these swords can pump out respectable damage numbers. This is especially got against Armored Foes. While Armored Units can't be Broken via Weapon Triangle Advantage, their Resistance stats are so low that the Levin Sword will shred through them like paper, even without Break Defenses activating. 

For this build, I recommend using a <b>Sword Mage Knight or Sword Griffin Knight</b> as both classes are Mixed Attackers with good Strength and Magic Stats. While Cavalry and Flying Units don't get any bonuses from Marth, this build more than makes up for it.  

</details>

# **Final Thoughts** <br>
I consider Marth to be the "Training Wheels" Emblem of the game. What I mean by this is that Marth shines brightest in the early game when your army isn't that strong and you have limited resources and Emblems to work with. His kit isn't all that complex and can be boiled down to "Do More Damage, Dodge More Attacks." This is great during the early game as you will need the damage and evasion Marth provides. For the first Emblem you receive, Marth accomplishes his job of teaching you the game's various mechanics with Emblems. Unfortunately, he falls off by the endgame as he's nowhere near the powerhouse he was in the early game, though he is still useful. This isn't a bad thing as by that time, he's served his purpose.
