# **Introduction**
Edelgard, Dimitri, and Claude are complicated Emblems (And no, I'm just talking about the Discourse). Like the Azure Twins who were 2 Emblems for the price of 1, the Fodlan Lords are 3 Emblems crammed into a single Bracelet. Unlike the Azure Twins, you don't get to control which Emblem is leading. Befitting their complicated status, the Fodlan Lords are designed to boost the overall combat abilities of their bearer, but unlike say, Marth, Roy, or the Azure Twins, they are nowhere near as straightforward. 


# DLC Emblems Mechanics
Compared to the Base Game Emblems, DLC Emblems function slightly differently. They all share the following traits.

<ul>
<li>DLC Emblems have all 20 of their Bond Levels available from the moment you acquire them. This means the profiles for DLC Emblems will be structured slightly differently to reflect this. </li><br>

<li>All DLC Emblems (except the Emblem of Heroes) have 2 versions of their respective Engage Attacks. The upgraded or + version is triggered when an Ally wearing a specific Base Game Emblem Ring is standing next to the user when they initiate the Engage Attack. </li><br>

<li>For Skill Inheritance of DLC Emblems their Stat and Weapon Skills work differently compared to Base Game Emblems. Their Stat Skills boost 2 Stats while their Weapon Skills are defensive "Guard" Skills that decrease damage against specific Weapon Types. </li>
</ul>

<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Friendly Rivalry</b> ("Friendly", yeah right) is similar to the Azure Twins' Night and Day Skill. The difference is that instead of being an option on the command menu, it triggers automatically. At the beginning of every Player Phase, this skill will randomly switch which of the Fodlan Lords is in the lead. This is significant as like Eirika and Ephraim, whichever Lord is currently leading affects two (2) of their Sync Skills, one of which is covered in this section. One thing to remember is that it is possible to get the same Lord two Turns in a row when Friendly Rivalry triggers. <br><br>

Lastly, this Skill deactivates the moment you enter the Fodlan Lords' Engage Form. Whichever Lord was leading at the time is locked in for the next 3-4 Turns. This might sound like you need to wait for the right Lord to tag in before Engaging but in practice, I find that when Engaged it doesn't matter who was leading. Once Engaged, you will have access to all of the Fodlan Lords' Engage Skills and Engage Weapons which we'll discuss later. <br><br>

The <b>Gambit Skills</b> are a command you will always have access to when Synced with the Emblem(s) of Rivals. Which Gambit Skill you can use depends on which Fodlan Lord is currently leading. This applies even when Engaged so be mindful of that.

<ul>
<li><b>Edelgard's Flame Gambit</b> creates Flame terrain in a 3x3 square grid around the targeted enemy. Unlike Roy's Blazing Lion, this is not an AoE-style attack. Only the single enemy targeted by this Skill will be damaged. If you recall back in Corrin's Profile, how much I gushed about Flame terrain being incredibly useful. Having another method of creating that kind of terrain is always appreciated.</li><br>

<li><b>Dimitri's Shield Gambit</b> can be thought of as a watered-down version of Lucina's Bonded Shield. It only works against ranged weapons (Bows, Daggers, Tomes, etc.). This can be useful if you want to bait out dangerous ranged foes. </li><br>

<li><b>Claude's Poison Gambit</b> has a similar degree of utility to Flame Gambit. In previous Profiles I've explained why Poison is good in Engage and Claude is one of the few ways in Engage to poison multiple targets in a single action. If you have a bunch of enemies frozen by Augmented Freeze or Dreadful Aura, slap on some Poison to rub more salt in the wounds. Even better, if you're fighting a tough Boss and Claude is currently leading, definitely use this to soften them up.</li><br>
</ul>

While these Gambit Skills have their utility, Friendly Rivalry randomly switching with Lord is leading, and thus which Gambit you can use makes it difficult to plan an effective strategy around them. It's a good idea to treat the Gambits as an afterthought. If you have the right Gambit for a given situation, that's great. If not, don't get too broken up over it. 

</details>

While the multiplier is much weaker than the likes of Marth's Mercurius or Lucina's Parthia, the tradeoff is that Lineage will always be active, even in the Arena once you hit Bond 3. <br><br> 

<b>Weapon Sync(+)</b> looks simple on paper but in practice is much more complicated. <b>To get that Attack boost, you need to match Weapon Types with whichever Fodlan Lord is leading</b>. If Edelgard is leading, you need to use an Axe. The same concept applies to Dimitri (Lances) and Claude (Bows). This means to get the most out of Weapon Sync, you need to pay attention to which Lord is leading. However, once Engaged, you get the bonus, regardless of Weapon Type. <br><br>

The Engage Weapons granted by the Emblem(s) of Rivals are <b>Aymr, Areadbhar</b>, and <b>Failnaught</b>. You might recognize these Engage Weapons as a few of the weapons provided by Byleth. Last but not least, we have <b>Failnaught</b> which I described previously as a <b>Longbow on Steroids</b>. This is great for shooting down pesky Fliers or attacking from 3 Range to avoid taking a hit.

<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Combat Arts</b> is a gameplay mechanic returning from Three Houses. However, since Engage lacks Weapon Durability, the cost to use these Combat Arts is "<b>Engage Turns</b>". What this means is that in exchange for accessing their powerful attacks/abilities, <b>you have to give up the time you can spend in the Fodlan Lord's Engage Form</b>. <br><br>

For example, if you only have 1 Turn left in their Engage Form and you use a Combat Art that costs one (1) Engage Turn, you'll be left with 0 Engage Turns. <b>Fortunately, being left with 0 Engage Turns doesn't immediately cancel the transformation. You will remain Engaged with the Emblem(s) of Rivals for the incoming Enemy Phase. On your next Player Phase, the Engage will end</b>. <br><br>

<b>Another caveat with the Combat Arts is that you must use the corresponding Engage Weapon</b>. The saving grace is that you have access to all 3 Combat Arts, even if you have not unlocked the corresponding Engage Weapon. This means even someone at Bond 1 with the Emblem(s) of Rivals, will still have all the Combat Arts while Engaged. <br><br>

<b>Edelgard's Raging Storm</b> is the most powerful and fittingly comes with the highest price of 3 Engage Turns. Even if you miss the attack, Raging Storm will still grant you another action as if you were refreshed by a Dance. Keep in mind that since Aymr is a Smash Wapon, you will attack second in combat and likely take a hit from the enemy. <br><br>

<b>Dimitri's Atrocity</b> is the most straightforward and has a decent price. When the skill says "Double Might" it means <b>Areadbhar's Might jumps from 14 to 28 when using Atrocity</b>. This makes it have power on par with Smash Weapons and makes it a nice "Kill One Enemy Button", especially if you land a Critical Hit. <br><br>

<b>Claude's Fallen Star</b> is more defensive in nature. It makes you invincible against the next enemy that attacks you after using it. The problem is that since the Skill forces you to use Failnaught, the target must be 2-3 spaces away. <br><br>

Lastly, please keep in mind that you can only use each Combat Art once per turn and only if you have Engage Turns left to pay the cost. For example, let's say you're at Bond 15 with the Emblem(s) of Rivals where you have 4 Turns while Engaged. If you used Raging Storm followed by Atrocity and you're refreshed by an ally (either Dance or Goddess Dance), you cannot use Fallen Star since you will not have any Engage Turns to pay the cost.


</details><br>

<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Houses Unite</b> is shared among the 3 Lords and is a better version of Leif's Quadruple Hit. Even though you're only attacking 3 times at 50% Damage, all 3 of the Engage Weapons used are still strong, even with the damage penalty. Since you have to be Engaged to use Houses Unite, Weapon Sync will always activate to boost the damage. Furthermore, Houses Unite's hits even harder is that 4 out of the 8 Class Types in Engage increase this attack's damage even further. <br><br>

<b>What makes Houses Unite truly broken is the bonus effect it receives if an ally wearing Byleth's Ring is standing next to them</b>. <b>Houses Unite+</b> grants another action after the Engage Attack and this additional action is separate from the one granted by Raging Storm. Keep in mind that if Byleth is around, then they could have used Goddess Dance on you, granting yet another additional action for <b>a total of 5 Attacks in a single turn</b>. Here's an example where Unit A is Synced with the Emblem(s) of Rivals and Unit B is Synced with Byleth.

<ol>
<li>Unit A uses Raging Storm and is given an additional action.</li>
<li>Unit A uses that additional action to attack again.</li>
<li>Unit A is refreshed by your Dancer for another action and attack.</li>
<li>Unit B uses Goddess Dance on Unit A.</li>
<li>Unit A (adjacent to Unit B) uses Houses Unite+ to attack and gain another action.</li>
<li>Unit A attacks one last time.</li>
</ol>

If you can set this up right, you could potentially wipe out multiple enemies with a single Unit, or destroy multiple HP bars on a Boss in a single turn.


</details>

For Skill Inheritance, the Fodlan Lords lean toward the more expensive side of the spectrum as the majority of their Skills will run you a pretty SP penny. However, there are some affordable options thrown in the mix. <br>

<ul>
<li><b>Assembly Gambit</b> is the only Gambit that can be accessed via Skill Inheritance. The reason this skill exists is because the main 3 Gambits rely on being Synced with one of the Fodlan Lords. Thus, the developers had to create a 4th Gambit for when a Unit isn't Synced with any of them. As for the Skill itself, it could be useful to pull enemies towards you or out of certain terrain. However, I rarely see this skill being used, even on Maddening. That 1500 SP price tag isn't doing this Skill any favors either. Fun Fact: Assembly Gambit is the only Sync Skill in Engage that is exclusive to Skill Inheritance. </li><br>

<li><b>Lineage</b> is fantastic as just like Byleth's Mentorship, the SP Cost is a steal for the benefits it provides. Like Sigurd's Canter, I recommend spreading this around your army so they all can get a boost to their EXP gain. If you get the Emblem(s) of Rivals early, you can combine this with Marth's Mercurius or Lucina's Parthia to make your EXP gain skyrocket.  </li><br>

<li><b>Weapon Sync(+)</b> is expensive and for good reason as that damage boost can make a world of difference. If you're like me and match Emblems to Units who specialize in their Weapon Type (e.g: Marth on a Sword Unit, Lyn on a Bow User, etc.) then you can consider Weapon Sync a universal damage boost even when not Engaged. I usually stick with the base version as that +5 Damage is more than enough in most situations.  </li><br>

<li>Their Stat Skill is <b>Strength/Dexterity+</b>. These are stats every Physical Unit wants more of but as we saw with Roy and his Strength+ Skills, any Skills that directly boost your raw damage are expensive.  </li><br>

<li>Their Weapon Skill is <b>Bow Guard</b>. This could be useful for Units who don't like fighting Bow enemies. I wouldn't recommend this on Fliers as the damage reduction isn't enough to negate the Effectiveness Bonus Bows have against Fliers.  </li><br>

<li>Lastly, for Weapon Proficiencies, the Fodlan Lords provide <b>Axe, Lance</b>, and <b>Bow Proficiencies</b>. This combination of Proficiencies opens up a wide range of Physical Reclass options for your army, including great classes like Wyvern Knight, Great Knight, and Warrior.   </li><br>

</ul>

# **Character Builds**




<details>
<summary> 2/3 Weapon Sync </summary>

The Emblem(s) of Rivals performs best on Physical Units, specifically <b>reliable all-rounder combat Units</b>. If you want to get the most out of them, <b>I'd recommend playing into Weapon Sync by putting the Emblem(s) of Rivals on Classes that wield multiple different Weapon Types, specifically those that correspond to the Fodlan Lords (Axes, Lances, Bows)</b>. I recommend the following Classes. <br>
<ul>
<li><b>Warriors</b> (Axes and Bows)</li>
<li><b>Wyvern Knight</b> (Axes and Lances)</li>
<li><b>Great Knight</b> (Axes and Lances)</li>
<li><b>Bow Knight</b> (Bows and Axes/Lances)</li>
</ul>

With these specific Classes and Weapon Combinations, you'll have access to 2 of the 3 Weapon Types that correspond to the Fodlan Lords. This means a 66.6% uptime on Weapon Sync. <b>I especially recommend Warrior and Wyvern Knight as they are fantastic classes in Engage</b>. Warrior gives you 3-Range Chain Utility with Failnaught while Wyvern Knight gives you Flier movement. Great Knight and Bow Knight are Cavalry Classes and get bonus damage from Houses Unite. <br><br>

Unfortunately, there are no Classes in Engage that wield Axes, Lances, and Bows all at the same time, so there is no way to have 100% uptime on Weapon Sync when not Engaged. 

</details>



# **Final Thoughts** <br>
The Emblem(s) of Rivals is quite strong, especially for the one DLC Emblem that is handed to you on a silver platter. While it may seem complicated at first glance, once you start using it, you'll find that it's a bit simpler to handle. It's a shame that the true extent of their power is locked behind Byleth whose Ring you don't get until the mid-game but the wait is worth it. For the best results, I suggest putting this Emblem on a strong, all-rounder Physical Unit. I guess all that discourse around Three Houses fuels this Emblem's monstrous strength.
