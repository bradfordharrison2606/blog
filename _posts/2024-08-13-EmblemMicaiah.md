# **Introduction**
Micaiah represents the 2nd Game of the Tellius Duology: **Radiant Dawn**. Unlike the previous 5 Emblems which focused primarily on combat, Micaiah is a **Support and Utility Emblem** focused on maximizing the power of Staves. This might sound like a letdown but let me assure you, Micaiah is often considered one of the strongest Emblems in the entire game, including DLC Emblems.

<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Cleric</b> is Micaiah's signature Sync Skill in the same vein as Marth's Perceptive and Roy's Hold Out. However, a major difference is that Cleric is NOT inheritable (hence why it isn't bolded), and for good reason. That Rank C limitation might sound like a letdown but there is a surprising number of Staves in Engage that are C Rank and below. This includes very useful Staves like <b>Physic, Restore, Obstruct, Freeze, Silence</b>, and most importantly, <b>Rewarp</b>. This is especially useful on Alear as they have access to the Convoy mid-battle and thus can pull Staves out to either assist your allies or hinder your enemies. Cleric means that even at Bond 1, Micaiah turns her wearer into a versatile Staff User and this is significant as Engage has many non-Mage characters who have decent Magic Stats and Growths.<br><br>

<b>Healing Light</b> might pale in comparison to Cleric but it is still a fantastic Sync Skill. It solves the long-standing issue of Fire Emblem, where staff users cannot heal themselves. I recommend spreading this skill around to your primary and even secondary Healers so they don't have to worry about keeping their HP topped off. This skill is especially useful on Qi-Adept Staff Classes like Martial Monk/Master as their Chain Guard ability requires them to be at full HP.<br><br>

Last, but not least is <b>Silence Ward</b> which is Simple, but Effective. Silence like in many RPGs prevents anyone afflicted from using magic or staves. This can be quite a pain if one of your Mages or Healers is affected by it and Micaiah acts as a nice barrier against that which lets her bearer continue aiding the army with staves and/or magic.


</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

Micaiah's Engage Form is one of the best in the game and <b>Augment</b> is only one of two reasons why. What this skill means when it says staves have "Area-of-Effect+1" is that staves now affect a cross/plus-shaped area. This is one of the few ways outside of the Fortify Staff to heal multiple allies at once. The Range+5 means that staves like Heal and Mend which normally, can only be used on adjacent allies, can be used on distant allies. Ranged staves like Physic have their range increased. <br><br>

However, don't think that Augment is limited to just healing staves. It works on <b>ALL</b> staves. This means you can have AoE versions of debilitating staves like <b>Silence, Freeze, Obstruct</b>, and <b>Entrap</b>. Entrap boosted by Augment is especially great as it allows you to pull up to 5 enemies out of position, separating them from their allies and breaking their formation. But the fun doesn't stop there, for this is only the tip of the Augment Iceberg. <br><br>

Augment is at its most powerful (read: Broken) when combined with mobility staves like <b>Warp, Rewarp</b>, and <b>Rescue</b>. Normally these staves only affect one ally and have a short range of between 5 - 8 spaces, limiting their utility. However, Augment not only boosts their range significantly but the AoE effect allows up to 5 members of your army to teleport halfway across the map. When you get the Dancer of this game, Seadall, you can have him refresh Micaiah's user for another round of Augmented teleportation. <br><br>

I cannot stress how ludicrously powerful this is. The ability to teleport your entire army across the map in a single turn can trivialize, if not completely invalidate some Chapters, especially if you know what you're doing. The main downside with Augment is that you can only use it for 3 - 4 Turns at a time before needing to recharge your Engage Meter.<br><br>

I'm not going to say much about the Class Bonuses as they are mostly irrelevant. You don't need them when the base effect Augment is this good.


</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Great Sacrifice</b> (GS) provides more evidence as to why support abilities can be completely busted. A full heal that covers the entire map for both your allies and NPCs (Green Units) is incredible in a pinch where multiple characters need healing. That penalty of reducing the user's HP to 1 might make it seem dangerous but logically speaking, you will likely keep Micaiah's user out of enemy range. Not to mention you have Healing Light to get yourself healthy again or you could have another unit heal Micaiah's user right after using it. <br><br>

However, what makes GS truly incredible is that it gives <b>A LOT OF EXPERIENCE</b>! How GS works when calculating EXP is that it's based on how many units were healed by it, no matter how small the amount. If your entire army was healed by GS, that's basically a free Level Up. This is useful as Engage is a game that heavily discourages level grinding through various means (Mainly slapping Void Curse on infinite reinforcements) but GS is one of the few ways of grinding in this game. Stick Micaiah on a low-level character for a chapter or two and watch their Level skyrocket as they use GS. <br><br>

Like Augment, the Unit Bonuses aren't worth discussing as they are arguably even more irrelevant than Augment's. The base effect and EXP gain of GS are just that good. 


</details>

<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Shine</b> has comparable stats to Celica's Seraphim but lacks the Corrupted Effectivness. Instead, Shine acts as an Illume/Torch Staff in weapon form. After attacking with Shine, it leaves behind a ball of light that lights up a 5x5 space diamond-shaped area in Fog of War. For the uninitiated, Fog of War (FoW) is a mechanic that covers the entire battlefield in darkness where the only thing you can see is the terrain. Enemies are completely hidden in the darkness and the main way to increase your visibility is through Torches or the Illume Staff. This sounds nice in theory but in practice, Shine has issues. The biggest issue is that FoW Maps are rare in Engage. There are only 3 FoW Maps in the Main Story (one of which Micaiah is unavailable for) and FoW Skirmishes are random. <br><br>

<b>Nosferatu</b> is more practical as it drains the enemy of their HP. This can be handy for getting Micaiah's user healthy again after using Great Sacrifice, doubly so if the target is an Armored Unit that will be taking a lot of damage (and you recover more HP) from magic. <br><br>

Micaiah's Engage Weapons aren't bad by any means. However, the issue lies in that they are overshadowed by Micaiah's Stave Utility. Micaiah is designed to be a Support Emblem, not a Combat Emblem. While you could use her Engage Weapons, you'll get far better mileage out of focusing on Augment and Great Sacrifice. 

</details>

<ul>
<li>
On your <b>Dancer, Seadall</b>. Seadall is a Support Unit who doesn't fight and likes to avoid direct combat at all costs. I recommend putting this Engrave on a <b>Shielding Art Weapon</b>. This Body Art is purely defensive in nature as it gives +5 DEF when equipped. Combine that with the -1 Weight, +40 Avoid, and +20 Dodge and you have added some serious bulk to your Dancer.</li><br>

<li>
On your <b>Dodge Tanks</b>. If you are running a Dodge Tank Build on a character, then this Engrave goes a long way. Not only for the boost to Avoid and Dodge but also for Weight Fixing, which lowers the chances of getting weighed down by a heavy weapon. </li>

<summary> Explanation </summary>

Contrasting Emblems like Roy whose Skill Inheritance is stupidly expensive, Micaiah's Skill Inheritance is dirt cheap across the board as the number of skills that will run you above 1000 SP can be counted on one hand. Unfortunately, <b>Cleric is not Inheritable</b>. <br>

<ul>

<li>
<b>Silence Ward</b> and <b>Healing Light</b> are the main prizes here. They are very useful for your dedicated Magic users and Healers. While you might not always have the room to keep them equipped, it's always nice to have these options in your back pocket based on your needs for the current map. I recommend having Silence Ward on your main Staff User beside Micaiah's user so they will always be able to use the Restore Staff to cure your other magic users of Silence. </li><br>

<li>
Micaiah's Weapon Skill is <b>Staff Mastery</b> and it's a little peculiar. The Hit+ bonus mainly affects Status Staves (Silence, Fracture, Entrap, etc.) which have hit rates. If you want to combine Micaiah's Augment with Status Staves, I recommend inheriting a Staff Mastery Skill onto Micaiah's main user to boost their chances of success as that +10/15 to Hit goes a long way. </li><br>

<li>
<b>Resistance+</b> is Micaiah's Stat Skill and it's rather underwhelming. While you think these skills are a way to patch Armored Unit's main weakness to Magic, you'd be wrong. Armored Unit's low Resistance, combined with their low Speed, means Mages routinely double them which renders any Resistance boost almost meaningless. You could put these skills on your main magical tank, to make them more resistant (pun intended) to magical attacks. </li><br>

<li>
Lastly, for Proficiencies, Micaiah gives three (3), a rarity for any (Non-DLC) Emblem not named Leif. She grants <b>Staves, Knives, and Tomes</b>. Micaiah and Leif are the only two Base Game Emblems to grant Staff Proficiency, and Micaiah gives it much earlier at Bond 3 compared to Leif at Bond 6. Tome Proficiency is nice but Celica gives it earlier compared to Micaiah. Knife Proficiency might seem out of place as Micaiah isn't even a Physical-based Emblem but it's a reference to her home game where 1) Knives were their own, separate Weapon Type and 2) Her closest companion and confidant Sothe used Knives as his primary weapon. </li>

</ul>

</details>

# **Character Builds**




<details>
<summary> Great Grinding</summary>

Micaiah is one of the few ways to level grind in Engage and that's via spamming Great Sacrifice. If you want to get the most mileage out of this, you are going to need outside assistance from two other Emblems you receive later in the game. The Ring of the Crux of Fate allows you to create Flame tiles that damage your allies so Micaiah's users can heal them for free EXP. The Ring of the Instructor grants a Sync Skill that boosts the EXP gained by adjacent allies.

</details>

<details>
<summary> Emergency Staff Services </summary>

If you are on a Map (Story, Paralogue, Skirmish, etc) with a limited number of deployment slots and you need an extra healer, it may be worth putting Micaiah on someone. Even at Bond 1, Cleric will still allow them access to most general-use healing staves. My personal favorite way of doing this is putting Micaiah on a <b>Mage Knight</b> as they are a fantastic combat class and adding Micaiah's Staff Utility to that makes them a fantastic all-rounder.


</details>


<details>
<summary> Divine Entrapment</summary>

If you want to lean more towards combining Augment with Status Staves, then I highly recommend inheriting the following Skills <br>
<ul>
<li> A <b>Staff Mastery</b> Skill from Micaiah herself. </li>
<li> The <b>Divine Pulse (+)</b> Skill from the Emblem within the Ring of the Instructor. </li>
</ul>
These two skills work in tandem to ensure that you will rarely miss with Status Staves. Divine Pulse synergizes well with Micaiah's user as its success rate scales off your Luck Stat, which is boosted by wearing the Ring of the Dawn Maiden. 

</details>

<details>
<summary> God(dess) of Staves</summary>

If you want to go all in on Micaiah's Staff Utility, I'd recommend using her on Classes who already wield staves naturally. Most Staff wielding classes in Engage (with the exception of Griffin Knights and Royal Knights) have a default Staff Rank of at least B (not considering Blue Proficiencies). This means that even at Bond 1, they can still perform most of the tricks with Augment such as mass teleportation and AoE debilitation. Staff users in Engage are already good but Micaiah elevates them to God Status. 

</details>


# **Final Thoughts** <br>
While Micaiah lacks the raw combat power of her fellow Emblems, she more than makes up for it with her Staff and Support-related abilities. She enables some truly disgusting strategies through her Augmented Staff abilities. Upon reflection, she's probably the reason the game doesn't hand you staves like Warp, Rescue, and Entrap on a silver platter until much later.

Combat-wise, Micaiah isn't bad by any means (She's certainly better than Leif, but that's not a high bar to clear), it's just her support abilities eclipse them in usefulness. I don't blame you if forget Micaiah even has Engage Weapons in the first place. **Basically, if you're using Micaiah for combat, you're using her wrong**.
