# **Introduction**
Alear represents the 2nd Switch Era Fire Emblem Game: **Engage**. Befitting their status as the final Emblem you obtain in the base game, Alear is designed to turn their user into a Combat Powerhouse. They are designed to exterminate **Bosses, Corrupted**, and most importantly; **Fell Dragons**. Their raw power is directly tied to the bonds they share with their allies. 

# **Engage+**

Because Alear is a controllable character instead of an equippable Emblem Ring, they work much differently compared to their fellow Emblems. To use Alear's Engage Form, simply move Alear adjacent to an allied Unit and select "**Engage+**" from the command menu. This will put both Alear and the targeted ally (Whom I will refer to as Alear's Engage Partner) into Alear's Engage Form **at the cost of only Alear's Engage Meter**. 

Once Alear has access to Engage+, they will always have an Engage Meter, even if they aren't Synced with an Emblem. This means you will always have access to Engage+.

Like other Emblems, the Stat Bonuses and Sync Skills Alear grants to both themselves and their Engage Partner are determined by Alear's Bond Level with their Engage Partner. However, where Alear differs is that their Bond Level is not raised by fighting or using Bond Fragments. Instead, **Emblem Alear's Bond Level is determined by Alear's Support Rank with their Engage Partner**.

<summary> Explanation </summary>

With Emblem Alear, "Sync Skills"  is a bit of a misnomer as you cannot Sync with Alear. Either you are Engaged with them or not. There is no middle ground. However, for gameplay purposes, the game treats these skills as Sync Skills. <b>Do keep in mind that whenever you enter Alear's Engage Form, Emblem Alear's Sync Skills will override whatever Sync Skills provided by Emblems both Alear and their Engage Partner have equipped</b>. This doesn't apply to inherited Skills though. <br><br>

<b>Holy Aura</b> is Alear's signature Sync Skill. When it says "Unit is Effective: Fell Dragon", it means that <b>ANY weapon either Alear or their Engage Partner use will have triple Might against Fell Dragons, mainly Corrupted Wyrms</b>. However, Holy Aura also fixes an issue I've been bringing up consistently in these profiles. <br><br>

Remember how I kept harping on Dragon Effective Weapons in previous profiles? Well, once you have Emblem Alear and Holy Aura, those weapons (<b>Falchion, Mulagir, Parallel Falchion, Byleth's Engage Weapons, etc.</b>) become much more useful, especially against the Final Boss of Engage. Yes, it took 22 Chapters but you finally have an answer to those pesky Corruped Wyrms.<br><br>

<b>Bond Forger(+)</b> is great for improving your army's accuracy and dodge-tanking capabilities. By the time you have access to this Skill, you have at least 12 Emblems at your disposal (Assuming you aren't using DLC) and 14 Deployment Slots for the rest of the Main Story Chapters. This means the majority of your army benefits from this skill, whether you realize it or not. <br><br>

Last but certainly not least is <b>Holy Shield</b>. This Skill is a fantastic defensive tool as not only does it negate Effectiveness Damage to whatever class you have Alear in, but it also negates their innate weakness to Dragon-slaying weapons. This Skill is especially great on Flying Units as it negates their primary weaknesses to Bows and Wind Magic.

<summary> Explanation </summary>

Alear's <b>Attuned</b> is simple but incredibly powerful as it encourages you to go on the offensive during the Player Phase with both Alear and their Engage Partner. This skill works similarly to Lyn's Alacrity and Speedtaker where they feed into each other. One member of the duo defeats an enemy, which then powers up the other member. The 2nd member of the duo uses their powered-up stats to defeat another enemy and give their partner the stat boost as well. These stat boosts last for the entire Engage (3-4 Turns). <b>Unfortunately, the stat boosts don't stack</b>, so you can't abuse Dances to get multiple kills in a single turn to stack them. <br><br>

The Class Bonuses are based on the Unit Type of whoever in the Alear Engage Duo who initiates combat. For example, say you have <br>

<ul>
<li> Alear as a Divine Dragon (<b>Dragon Unit</b>) </li>
<li> Chloe as a Griffin Knight (<b>Flying Unit</b>) </li>
</ul>

If Alear activities Attuned, then Chloe with get +5 to all her stats due to Alear's Dragon Class Bonus. Next, if Chloe activates Attuned, then Alear will receive +4 to all their stats along with the extra +2 Resistance from Chloe's Flying Class Bonus. <br><br>

This makes Attuned an incredibly versatile Engage Skill as everyone gets stronger from this skill along with their respective Class Bonuses. <br><br>

<b>If you want to make the most of this skill, make sure both Alear and their Engage Partner are decent Combat Units to make killing enemies easier</b>. Fortunately, this isn't too hard given Alear's Engage Weapons which we'll discuss later. For now, let's move on to Alear's Engage Attack. 

</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

Continuing Alear's streak of being a unique Emblem is their Engage Attacks, yes plural. Alear has 2 Engage Attacks (<b>Dragon Blast</b> & <b>Bond Blast</b>) and which one you get depends on whether or not the dynamic duo are adjacent. <br><br>

<b>The main issue with Dragon/Bond Blast is the Magic Beam attack</b>. Since it's magic-based, it suffers from the same problem as Leif's Quadruple Hit. To maximize the damage of these attacks, you need both Alear and their Engage Partner to have decent Strength and Magic Stats which isn't always the case. Despite this, Dragon/Bond Blast is nowhere near as useless as Quadruple Hit. Let's talk about these talks individually. <br><br>

<b>Dragon Blast</b> while powerful, usually isn't enough to deplete a Boss's HP bar from full unless they've been debuffed by Corrin's Draconic Hex. However, it is good for softening up Bosses so your other Units can kill them. Remember that enemies cannot counterattack when you use Engage Attacks. <br><br>

<b>Bond Blast</b>, however, is a completely different story. Unless Alear and their Engage Partner are severely underpowered or are fighting something incredibly bulky, expect this attack to do insane amounts of damage. If you leverage the Dragon Bonus of this attack, expect to see damage numbers reaching the high 90s and even breaking into triple digits. <b>In short, Bond Blast is almost guaranteed to one-shot an enemy's HP bar</b>. <br><br>

The way I use these two Engage Attacks is on Bosses to quickly shred through their HP bars. <b>First, you inflict Draconic Hex on the target to lower their stats (mainly their Defense and Resistance). Next, you use Dragon Blast to soften up the target for another Unit to finish off, or even better, destroy that HP Bar. Afterward, you use Bond Blast to completely destroy another HP Bar</b>. <br><br>

If you want to pull off this combo with relative ease, <b>it's a good idea for either Alear or their Engage Partner to have inherited Sigurd's Canter</b>. After one of them uses Dragon Blast on the Boss, they can move 1 space to the side so the other member of the dynamic duo can come in right next to their Engage Partner and initiate Bond Blast for massive damage. <br><br>

Lastly, <b>the Class Bonuses only apply to the Unit that INITIATES the Engage Attack</b>. This mainly applies to Bond Blast. For the Sword attack, while you could use a Smash Weapon as I always recommend, Alear has you more than covered with their Engage Weapons.


</details>

<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Lyration</b> is an upgrade to Alear's signature weapon, Libération. As such, it can be thought of as Alear's signature Engage Weapon in a similar vein to Marth's Falchion or Ike's Ragnell. This weapon is great as it has stats comparable to your average Bond 15 Weapon. It doesn't stop there as its ability to fill up adjacent allies' Engage Meter lets you pull off some crazy combos if you know what you're doing. All it takes is some clever positioning of your Unit and both members of the Alear Engage Duo. <br><br>

If you're curious about the name, Lyration is believed to be a portmanteau of "<b>Lilac</b>" and "<b>Relation</b>". Lilac Flowers are usually purple which is a mix of Red and Blue, referencing Alear's hair and dual nature. Furthermore, in the Japanese Language of Flowers, Lilacs can represent many things, including Friendship which fits Emblem Alear both thematically and gameplay wise. <br><br>

<b>Dragon's Fist</b> is another good Engage Weapon. Unlike other Arts Weapons, this one is purely Magic-based, meaning any Magical Units Engaged with Alear have a great combat option for themselves. Even non-Mages could use this to exterminate Armored foes. <br><br>

<b>Oligoludia</b> at Bond 20 doesn't disappoint either. Despite the 8 Might, this weapon is incredible for the same reason as Eirika's Sieglinde; its <b>Corrupted Effectiveness</b>. By the time you have access to this weapon, EVERY SINGLE generic enemy in the remaining Story Chapters is Corrupted. This means Oligoludia effectively has 24 Might 99% of the time. This makes Oligoludia great for exterminating Corrupted and getting the stat boost from Attuned. This weapon's name origin is a bit harder to pin down but most sources agree its name is derived from the Greek saying "<b>Oi evgenikoí lígoi</b>" which translates to "<b>The Noble Few</b>". <br><br>

Lastly, we have the <b>Dragon Beam</b>. It isn't called this in-game or even has a name but I'm putting it here for completeness. It's another "Engage Attack Exclusive" weapon and is powerful with 20 Might. However, as stated previously, unless Alear and/or their partner have good Magic stats, don't expect a lot of damage from this thing when using their Engage Attack. 

</details>

Aside from the usual Support bonuses, if Alear Engages with this character, both of them will be granted the only Bond 21 Sync Skill in the entire game: <b>Boon of Elyos</b>. <br><br>

Boon of Elyos encourages you to have Alear and their S-Support attack last in a given turn. On Story Chapters with 14 Deployment Slots, <b>that's effectively a +26 to your Critical Rate</b> which is nice. When this skill says "number of allies who have acted this turn." it means everyone who has used their turn and cannot act (As in, their character model on the map is grayed out). You might think to leverage this on a weapon with a high Critical Rate like a Killer Weapon with a Critical boosting Engrave but at that point, your Critical Rate is already so high that Boon of Elyos doesn't contribute much. <br><br>

Because of how late it comes and its okay-ish effect, Boon of Elyos as a Skill is mostly an afterthought. It isn't game-changing like Ike's Wrath or Corrin's Pair Up so you won't see any heated debates/discussions on "Optimal S-Support Partners". This means you can have Alear S-Support, anyone, you want without losing much.

# **Character Tips**

I find Emblem Alear similar to Emblem Roy as they're a straightforward Emblem designed to make almost anyone a Combat Powerhouse for 3-4 Turns. Because of their unique mechanics where they can Engage with multiple different Units in a single map, I find it's better to go over some Rules of Thumb to get the most out of Emblem Alear, regardless of who their Engage Partner is at the moment.


<details>
<summary> Power of Friendship</summary>

Since Emblem Alear's raw strength is determined by their Support Level with the Engage Partner, it's a good idea to get Alear to an A-Support Rank with everyone in your main army. That way, Alear's Engage Form will be at its strongest (Bond 20) regardless of their Engage Partner. <br><br>

While this might sound like a tall order, fear not. If you've been diligently collecting resources around the Somniel for your entire adventure, you've likely stockpiled various gifts, including <b>Spirit Gems</b>. Spirit Gem is a gift that is universally liked by the entire playable cast and giving them one will boost Alear's Support level with them. If you keep spamming everyone with Spirit Gems, you'll have A-Support Ranks in no time. 

</details>

<details>
<summary> Engage Vs. Engage+</summary>

It would be wise to remember that you don't always have to default to Alear's Engage Form. If Alear is Synced with an Emblem, nothing is stopping you from Engaging with them instead. Despite Alear's Engage Form being extremely powerful, it is not a "one size fits all" solution and there are situations where Emblem Alear is not the answer. For example, if Alear has Lucina equipped, it might be better to Engage with Lucina for Bonded Shield instead of activating Engage+.

</details>


<details>
<summary> Timing. When to use Engage+</summary>

From my experience, <b>the best time to use Engage+ is at the beginning and very end of a Chapter. When you're about to fight the Boss</b>. Using it at the beginning allows for a powerful start to the chapter as Alear and their Engage Partner hit the front lines of the enemy hard. By the time you reach the Boss at the end of the Chapter, Alear's Engage Meter should be refilled for another round of Engage+. <br><br>

Alternatively, if there is a Corrupted Wyrm in your way and you can't avoid it or no one else can kill it in a single round, consider burning your Engage+ to exterminate it with Holy Aura. 


</details>

<summary> Canter.</summary>

If you want to get the most out of Bond Blast and Dragon Blast, then make sure that at least one member of the Alear Engage Duo has Canter. I recommend putting Canter on Alear as they are required for Engage+ and you don't have to spread around Canter to everyone in your main army. 
</details>


<details>
<summary> Favourite Food</summary>

The Sync Skill Favorite Food from Celica fully refills your Engage Meter when you eat a Packed Lunch. On Alear, it means they can use Engage+, wreak havoc for 3-4 Turns, and then recharge their Engage Meter by taking their government-mandated lunch break. This is great if you're about to fight the Boss of a Chapter and Alear's Engage Meter hasn't recharged yet and there is no Emblem Energy around. 

</details>

# **Final Thoughts** <br>

As the final Emblem in the Main Story and your reward for gathering all 12 Emblem Rings, I find Emblem Alear more than worth it. As Alear's "<b>Super Mode</b>" they more than deliver by turning themselves and their Engage Partner into Combat Powerhouses. Once you have this Emblem, few things can stand up to the sheer power of a fully realized Divine Dragon and The Miracle granted by the 12 Emblems. Use the powers of the 13th Emblem wisely, and watch your enemies fall one after the other until nothing is standing between you and Sombron.
