# **Introduction**
Corrin represents the 2nd of the 3DS Fire Emblem Games: **Fates**. On first impression, Corrin is a... peculiar Emblem whose role is often misunderstood by first-time/uniformed players. However, once you dissect her kit, you realize Corrin is a Support Emblem designed to make life miserable for the enemy through **debilitating debuffs and altering the very terrain of the battlefield**.

Corrin's signature Sync Skill is **Dragon Vein** and it is perhaps the most complicated Skill to explain. Hence why it gets its own subsection. Dragon Vein allows you to create unique terrain effects. Before I go any further, I need to make one thing crystal clear. **Terrain effects created through Dragon Vein DO NOT DISCRIMINATE**! What I mean is that any terrain you create affects both sides equally. The positive effects can help the enemy and the negative effects can harm you, The Player. So please keep that in mind when using Dragon Vein. <br>

Moving on, what makes Dragon Vein unique among Sync Skills is that it's basically a Pseudo-Engage Skill. Every Unit Type in the game produces a different kind of terrain. The following section details the different Veins granted to the different Unit Types.

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

As you can see, Corrin has many different Dragon Vein effects to offer. Some are better than others, but they all have some level of utility. <br><br>

<b>Backup Units can add some extra Defense and Resistance to your units</b>. This can be useful, especially on the Enemy Phase as that increased Defense can potentially reduce the enemy damage to 0 and prevent them from Breaking one of your Units. However, if you want to avoid being Broken, there is another terrain effect that does a much better job. <br><br>

<b>Armored Units can share their immunity to Break with their allies </b>. This can be great if you want to tank on the Enemy Phase without worrying about being Broken via Weapon Triangle Disadvantage. While you don't get a DEF/RES bonus, this is a more reliable way of avoiding Break. <br><br>

<b>Cavalry Units and their Water terrain is a double-edged sword</b>. Water tiles not only reduce Avoid but also slows down movement. Unfortunately, because Corrin's terrain doesn't discriminate, this can harm you more than the enemy. The checkerboard placement of the effect can do more bad than good if you take some consideration when aiming it. <br><br>

<b>Covert Units get the most "braindead" effect with Fog</b>. Covert Units get the Avoid bonus from Fog tiles doubled, making them borderline untouchable. In the early days of Engage, this made players put Corrin on Thieves and call it a day.<br><br>

<b>Flying Units get some healing utility with their Healing Glow tiles</b> which can be nice if your designated healers aren't in range. <br><br>

<b>Mystical Units create Flame tiles</b> and this is my personal favorite of Corrin's Dragon Veins. These Flame tiles are the same kind of flames created by Roy's Blazing Lion, except you always have access to them. Flame tiles are great for slowing down and keeping dangerous enemies at bay by severely limiting their movement, all while damaging them. If a horde of enemies is heading your way, just lay down some of Corrin's flames to buy you a turn or two to prepare for them. <br><br>

Unfortunately, <b>Qi Adepts get the worst effect Corrin has to offer</b>. While the Ice Pillars can be useful to hinder enemy movement, they are the only terrain Corrin produces that is destructible. If you want to slow down enemies, the Flame tiles do a better job. <br><br>

However, if you can't pick just one, you don't have to because Dragon Units get access to <b>ALL of Corrin's Dragon Vein options</b>. If you decide to keep Alear in their Dragon Child/Divine Dragon Class, Corrin makes them an even better Support Unit. 


</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

While not as versatile as Dragon, Corrin's other Sync Skills still bring plenty of value to the table. <br><br>

<b>Quality Time</b> can be great for keeping your army topped off on HP. Increase Supports is nice and all but the amount of points it provides is often negligible. This skill works well when combined with Sigurd's Canter as it can put you in a better position to heal allies after acting. <br><br>

Corrin's best Sync Skill (arguably even better than Dragon Vein) is <b>Draconic Hex</b>. Inflicting -4 on an enemy's basic stats (<b>Strength, Magic, Dexterity, Speed, Luck, Defense, and Resistance</b>) can absolutely cripple enemies and especially Bosses. While the stat penalties decrease every turn, usually the enemy will be long dead before you ever need to worry about that. There's also nothing stopping you from reapplying the stat penalties with Corrin. <br><br>

The best part about Draconic Hex is that <b>your attack doesn't even need to deal damage or land</b>. All you have to do is initiate combat and Draconic Hex will trigger. Pair this with a Thunder Tome or a Longbow and you can debuff enemies from 3 spaces away. All of this makes Draconic Hex one of my personal favorite Sync Skills in the entire game. 


</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

When Engaged, Corrin's role as a Debuffer is further emphasized with her Engage Skill <b>Dreadful Aura</b>. This Engage Skill not only inflicts Freeze on the enemy Corrin's bearer targets directly but also any enemies adjacent to the target. Not only will the enemy be stopped dead in their tracks, but they will also receive stat penalties via Draconic Hex (assuming you've unlocked it). Sadly, Draconix Hex doesn't spread to adjacent enemies like the Freeze effect. <br><br> 

<b>If you're about to fight a Boss, it's a good idea to have Corrin's bearer Engage and attack them first to nail them with Debuffs to soften them up considerably for the rest of your army to take down</b>. If you want to push Dreadful Aura to its limits, it is best combined with Corrin's Engage Attack. <br><br>

Fun Fact: Dreadful Aura is one of the very few Engage Skills in the game that doesn't grant a bonus to Dragon Units, but also grants bonuses to other Unit Types.

</details>

## Engage Attack

| ![Engage Attack Pic]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/corrin/FE17_Torrential_Roar_Icon.webp) <br> **Torrential Roar** |Use to attack 3 spaces in a line, turning those spaces into Water terrain.   |

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

<b>Torrential Roar</b> (Again, thanks Smash Bros.) is like Sigurd's Override as it attacks every enemy in a straight line with an upper limit of 4. This Engage Attack usually doesn't kill or even deal decent damage but that's okay. You're not using Torrential Roar for the damage. You're using it for the Debuffs. <br><br>

Assuming you're at least Bond 8 with Corrin, every enemy hit by Torrential Roar will also be affected by Draconic Hex and Dreadful Aura. Not only are you debuffing up to 4 enemies simultaneously, but freezing them and all adjacent enemies. The cherry on top is the Water terrain left behind which inflicts a -30 Avoid penalty that enemies can't negate by moving because they will be frozen. <br><br>

<b>One of the biggest perks of Torrential Roar is the Water terrain it leaves behind OVERRIDES the effects of the terrain previously there</b>. For example, say a Boss is on a Protective Tile that provides +30 Avoid. Torrential Roar's Water terrain and its -30 Avoid will cancel out the +30 Avoid granted by the Protective Tile. <br><br>

Corrin's kit up to this point has been varying levels of amazing. However, if you've been paying attention to these profiles, you would have noticed a trend. If an Emblem has great Sync Skills and/or Engage abilities, there is a good chance their Engage Weapons are their main drawback. Unfortunately, Corrin is no different. 


</details>

If a character is a little fragile and has low HP, these skills can add a decent bit of bulk to them or make your bulky units even bulkier. The major weakness of these skills is that more HP means Enemy Chain Attacks will do more damage since their damage scales off the target's maximum HP. <br><br>

<b>Sword Focus</b> isn't that great. I'd even say it's terrible as making yourself more susceptible to enemy Critical Hits in exchange for accuracy isn't worth it. Swords have high Hit Rates naturally, rarely dipping below 70% and most Sword Users and Classes have good Dexterity stats to ensure they rarely miss. <br><br>

Lastly, the only Weapon Proficiency Corrin grants is <b>Sword Proficiency</b> which is nothing new as plenty of other Emblems have this covered. This is a real shame because, in her home game, Corrin's default Class could be promoted into Classes that also used Tomes (Nohr Noble) or Staves (Hoshido Noble) depending on your in-game choices.

# **Character Builds**




<details>
<summary> Divine Dragon Vein</summary>

Unlike most other Lords in Fire Emblem, Alear is more of a Support Unit than a Combat powerhouse which makes them a fantastic partner for Corrin. If you're planning on keeping Alear in their <b>Dragon Child/Divine Dragon Class</b>, then they will get a lot of mileage out of Corrin's abilities. <br><br>

With this build, Alear will have full access to every single Dragon Vein Corrin has to offer. This adds a lot of versatility and flexibility to Alear as you can pick and choose which Dragon Vein effect is ideal for the current situation. <br><br>

Furthermore, since Alear leans towards Support, <b>having them open a Boss fight with Torrential Roar, frees up the rest of your army to go to town on their debilitated foe(s)</b>. 

</details>

<details>
<summary> Mystical Hoshidan Wildfire</summary>

This is my personal favorite way of running Corrin. I've already explained how good the Flame tiles created by <b>Mystical Units</b> are but it doesn't stop there. <br><br>

Mystical Units add more utility to Draconic Hex and Dreadful Aura. Most, if not all playable Mystical Classes have a Tome Rank high enough to use basic Thunder Tomes. This allows you to inflict Corrin's Debuffs from a safe 3 spaces away. Thunder Tomes can be inaccurate but remember that you don't need the attack to land to inflict Corrin's Debuffs. <br><br>

If you're going for this build, I recommend Corrin on Classes like <b>High Priest and Sage</b>. Both of these classes provide Staff Utility in addition to Corrin's healing via Quality Time but if you want Corrin's bearer to contribute more to combat in addition to Debuffing, then Sage is the way to go. 

</details>


<details>
<summary> Fog & Dagger</summary>

This build was ubiquitous when Engage first launched and the "meta" hadn't solidified yet. <b>Covert Units double the Avoid bonus from their Fog to +60 Avoid which drastically increases their dodge tanking capabilities</b>. <br><br>

In particular, the character <b>Yunaka</b> is the primary candidate for this build. Not only is she a Thief by default, but her Personal Skill (Trained to Kill) gives her +15 Critical when standing in terrain with an Avoid bonus, such as Corrin's Fog. <br><br>

While this build is quite useful and easy to put together, it has issues. This build is very easy to get addicted to and you'll end up ignoring Corrin's other Dragon Vein effects which might be more helpful for a given Chapter. <b>Secondly, if you're playing on Maddening Difficulty, having too much Avoid is a bad thing</b>. <br><br>

On Maddening the Enemy AI is programmed to deliberately ignore units they cannot damage (whether they deal 0 Damage to them, or the unit's Avoid is so high, they have 0 Accuracy). This can lead to enemies ignoring your Covert Unit in Fog and going for your other units. 


</details>

<details>
<summary> Nohrian Tank and Spank</summary>

If you want to cash in on the sheer bulk Corrin provides, then you can put Corrin on a Tank like a <b>General or Great Knight</b>. <br><br>

Generals provide the better Dragon Vein effect of the two by sharing their Break Immunity with their allies. Sword Focus could see use on Great Knights who use swords as their Dexterity can be lacking by the Endgame. <br><br> 

Lastly, both of these Classes have great Physical offenses so they'll get better mileage out of Torrential Roar. Even if it doesn't kill the enemies, it will soften them up immensely for your other units to finish off.

</details>


# **Final Thoughts** <br>
Corrin is a versatile Emblem that fills a unique niche among the roster. No other Emblem can inflict as many Debuffs and debilitating effects as she can so you should make it a priority to familiarize yourself with her ins and outs. On higher difficulties where Bosses can have inflated stat values, Corrin can cripple them and make it much easier to kill them. You should definitely take some time to play around with Corrin's different Dragon Vein effects to see which one fits your general play style. Lastly, if you're going to use Ike for Great Aether frequently, I highly recommend completing Corrin's Paralogue ASAP for access to Pair Up.
