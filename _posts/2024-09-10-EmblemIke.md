# **Introduction**
Ike (despite his appearance and gameplay suggesting otherwise) represents the 1st half of the Tellius Duology: **Path of Radiance**. He is an Emblem whose entire game plan is based around **Tanking**. He is designed to turn his wielder into an incredibly durable Tank that not only absorbs insane amounts of damage but deals an insane amount of damage to his enemies in retaliation. Think of Ike as a "**Brick Wall that Hits Back.**"

<summary> Explanation </summary>

Like the Azure Twins, Ike has two Bond Level 1 Sync Skills and both are inheritable. <br><br>
	
<b>Demolish</b> is useful for breaking down destructible terrain as it ignores their HP. However, this skill is rather situational. Outside of Ike's debut Chapter, you rarely encounter destructible terrain that would necessitate this skill. <br><br>
	
<b>Resolve</b>, however, is much more useful. While it's nowhere near as crazy here as it was in Ike's home game, that boost to Defense and Resistance synergizes well with Ike's Engage Skill. That 75% or less HP Threshold is rather generous and easier to hit than you'd think. Don't be surprised if Resolve is constantly active on Ike's main bearer.<br><br>
	
<b>Reposition</b> is like Roy's Advance. It's a simple but useful mobility tool. In practice, by moving an ally to the opposite side of the user, that ally has effectively moved an extra 2 spaces. 



</details>

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>
Ike is rather unique in that Engaging with him radically changes the play style of his bearer because <b>Laguz Friend</b> will become active. This skill requires some context to fully understand the pros and cons. <br>

<ol>
<li>When the skill says "<b>Unit takes 1/2 Damage</b>", what it means is that the damage inflicted by an enemy is calculated normally, and THEN is cut in half.</li>
	
<li>Your Avoid being set to 0 doesn't mean you can't dodge attacks at all. What it means is that if an enemy has a Hit Rate below 100%, there is a chance (however small) that Ike's bearer can dodge.</li>
</ol>

Having every attack only hit half as hard means Ike's bearer can effectively take on twice as many enemies as they normally would. If you have a unit with good base Defense and Resistance, combined with the effects of Resolve, they could potentially stand up to both Physical and Magical enemies with an impressive amount of bulk. This is also great as you want to draw in as many enemies as possible for Ike's Engage Attack which we'll discuss in the following section. <br><br>
	
Laguz Friend's main weakness is <b>Chain Attacks</b>. As a reminder, Chain Attacks from Backup Units deal fixed damage equal to 10% of the target's Max HP and have a fixed accuracy of 80%. Because Chain Attacks ignore your Defense/Resistance Stats, Laguz Friend can't do anything about that. So be careful that your Ike User isn't chipped away by Chain Attacks until they inevitably die. But fear not, as there is a way to block Chain Attacks. It involves inheriting a Sync Skill from the Ring of the Crux of Fate which you obtain not too long after obtaining Ike himself.


</details>

| ![Lodestar Rush Pic]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/ike/FE17_Great_Aether_Icon.webp) <br> **Great Aether** | Use to grant DEF/RES+5, but unit can’t counter for 1 Turn. Next Turn, attack a 2-space area, heal HP=30% of Damage dealt. <br> Sword/Axe only. |

<details>
<summary> Explanation </summary>

The main idea behind <b>Great Aether</b> is that it turns Ike's bearer into a <b>Time Bomb that detonates the following turn</b>. Therefore, the best use of Great Aether is rushing into a horde of enemies, digging in for the Enemy Phase, and then retaliating with extreme prejudice the following turn. You might think that not being able to counterattack enemies for 1 Turn is a pain, but if you have the right weapons, Great Aether's detonation on the following turn MORE than makes up for it. <br><br>
	
Now you may think that having a single unit charge head-first into enemy lines is suicide, especially considering they'll be a sitting duck since they can't counterattack. But remember that to even use Great Aether, you have to be Engaged with Ike in the first place. This means Laguz Friend will also be active, giving you 50% Damage Reduction. This Damage Reduction stacks not only with the +5 DEF and RES Great Aether grants but also the effects of Resolve (once your HP gets low enough) and Ike's passive stat bonuses. When all these come together, don't be surprised if you're barely taking any damage while charging up Great Aether. Besides, any damage you would have taken will be healed when you strike on the following turn. <br><br>
	
Out of all the Engage Attacks in the game, Great Aether benefits the most from using Smash Weapons. If you want Great Aether to hit as hard as possible, consider using <b>Axes with insane base Might such as a Steel Greataxe (26 Might) or a Silver Greataxe (32)</b>. This is without factoring in Forge Refines or Engravings. If Ike's User has a high Strength Stat combined with a Greataxe, nothing short of Armored Enemies are going survive this. <br><br>
	
For maximum effect, you'll want to position Ike's bearer in an open area to draw in as many enemies as possible. Ideally, Great Aether will cover the range depicted in the diagram below. Even if Ike's bearer can't use high-power Axes normally, don't fret because Ike has them covered with his Engage Weapons. <br><br>

<summary> Explanation </summary>

Ike's Engage Weapons all follow the theme of "<b>Really Strong, but also Really Heavy.</b>". Ike's Engage Weapons are some of the heaviest in the game, meaning the majority of your army will have trouble double-attacking with them. Fortunately, unlike Leif's Master Lance, these weapons have the Might to match their Weight. Besides, even if these weapons weigh you down to the point where you get doubled, remember that Laguz Friend will be active to buffer a lot of the incoming damage. <br><br>
	
Ike's <b>Hammer</b> is very different from a regular Hammer. Firstly, it's a Smash Weapon, meaning you can't double attack and, you attack last if you initiate combat. To make up for this, Ike's Hammer has double the Might as a regular Hammer. Unlike Lyn's Killer Bow, I prefer Ike's Hammer to a regular one as the Effectiveness Bonus makes one-shotting Armored Units much easier. If you're familiar with memes in the Fire Emblem Community, specifically those around Ike and his infamous rival, The Black Knight, you'll know exactly why they gave Ike this weapon. <br><br>
	
<b>Urvan</b> at Bond 10 has the honor of being one the strongest non-Smash Axes in the game. The only other Axe that beats it is DLC-exclusive. 21 Might is insane and makes Urvan a good substitute for Greataxes when using Great Aether. Even if you're using it in regular combat, its high accuracy means it won't have a hard time hitting foes. 19 Weight is a bit excessive for a non-Smash weapon, but its other traits make it a fair trade-off. That boost to Resistance stacks well with Ike's other defensive bonuses.

</details>

Ike's <b>Radiance Engrave</b> functions similarly to Roy's Binding Engrave. Ike's Engrave has a higher Might boost and doesn't reduce your Avoid at the cost of an insane Weight penalty. You are not doubling with any weapon with a Radiance Engrave, not even a Slim Sword. <br><br>

The standard use case is putting this Engrave on Weapons that cannot double attack like <b>Smash Weapons</b> or <b>Thoron</b>. If you're leaning into Ike's Great Aether with a Greataxe, then this is the Engrave to use as Laguz Friend makes dodging borderline impossible, so that Weight penalty is moot when charging up Great Aether. <br><br>

Alternatively, you could run this on <b>Killer Weapons</b> as every extra point of Might is effectively +3 Might when you land a Critical Hit.

<summary> Explanation </summary>

Ike's Skill Inheritance is reasonably priced across the board which is fitting for someone like him. Ike's not the kind of guy to charge you an arm and a leg for his services. <br>

<ul>
<li>Out of all of Ike's Sync Skills, <b>Demolish</b> can be safely ignored and you'll lose nothing. Destructible Terrain is rare in Engage and just having Demolish on Ike's main bearer is more than enough.</li><br>
	
<li><b>Resolve</b> may be worth your time and SP. You could put this on units that see a lot of combat on the front lines as that boost to Defense and Resistance can make them surprisingly bulky. 1000 SP is not asking too much for that kind of defensive power.</li><br>
	
<li><b>Reposition</b> is an underrated mobility tool, much like Roy's Advance. If you can't decide what skill to punt on a unit, Reposition makes a decent candidate at a reasonable SP cost.</li><br>
	
<li>Ike's Stat Skill is <b>Defense+</b> which like Lyn's Speed+ is "Boring, but Practical". It's never a bad idea to have some extra Defense on a character, especially if they fight on the front lines.</li><br>

<li>His Weapon Skill is <b>Axe Power</b>. Just like Roy's Sword Power, these skills are really powerful but really expensive. It might be worth putting on your Axe-users like Warriors, Berserkers, or Great Knights if you don't care about that Avoid penalty. Alternatively, if you want to pump up Great Aether's damage, consider having Ike's bearer inherit one of these skills.</li><br>
	
<li>For Weapon Proficiencies, Ike provides <b>Axes</b> and <b>Swords</b>. These two Proficiencies together allow any unit to gain access to the Wyvern Knight Class which is one of the better Classes in Engage.</li> 
</ul>	

While Ike's Skill Inheritance may seem good initially, the real prize is locked behind his Paralogue. Once you complete Ike's Paralogue, you will have access to an Inheritable Sync Skill that is the key to Ike's true potential as an Emblem. Fortunately for the Player, Ike's Paralogue unlocks right after his debut Chapter. However, be warned that Ike's Paralogue is one of the more difficult ones in the game but don't let that deter you from trying to complete it as soon as it's available. 

</details>

# **Character Builds**

There are 2 Builds I usually run with Ike, both of which are fun to mess around with.

<details>
<summary> Vantage+Wrath Combo </summary>

This is the Bread and Butter Ike Build as it is quite easy to assemble as by the time you have Ike, you already have access to most of the necessary components. To make this build, you'll need the following components. <br>

<ol>
<li>A Unit with a <b>high Strength Stat and/or Growth Rate such as Louis, Amber, Panette, etc.</b> Reclass them into an Axe-wielding Class with a high Strength Class Growth like <b>Warrior or Berserker</b>.</li><br>
	
<li>The Unit selected in Step 1 has inherited <b>Vantage (or its upgraded forms)</b> from Leif. Louis and Amber are especially good candidates for this as by the time you have Leif to get Vantage, both of them have already joined your army. </li><br>
	
<li>A <b>Killer Axe with an Engraving that boosts Critical Rate</b>. While many Engraves boost Critical Rate, I prefer using Lyn's Engrave as not only does it boost Critical Rate but also Accuracy. How Engage works with Critical Hits is that it first checks to see if your attack will connect and THEN rolls to see if you'll land a Critical. This means having high accuracy is essential to this build. </li><br>
	
<li>You have <b>completed Ike's Paralogue</b> to unlock his higher Bond Levels and Wrath. </li><br>
</ol>	

A normal Killer Axe has a base Critical Rate of 30. Most Critical-focused Engraves give Critical Rate boosts of 20-30. Wrath gives a Critical Boost of 1-30 depending on the user's current HP. With all of these components in place, Ike's User can reach absurd Critical Rates in the 60s and above. This is before factoring in a unit's Dexterity which also boosts Critical Rate. <br><br>
	
With this build, Ike's bearer will tank hits which will activate Wrath to make their Critical Rate skyrocket. Once they are past a certain HP Threshold, Resolve will activate, adding a lot of bulk to the unit. At that point, Ike's bearer can shrug off enemy attacks and obliterate them with a Wrath-fueled Critical Hit. <br><br>
	
When your HP gets too low, Vantage will activate as an insurance policy. When Vantage activates, you will attack first and likely eviscerate them with a Critical Hit. Remember that regular Vantage requires you to be at 25% HP or less to activate. At that point, you are most likely receiving the maximum Critical Boost from Wrath (+30 Critical) so don't be surprised if you have a Critical Rate somewhere in the 80s or 90s. <br><br>
	
I cannot stress how powerful this build is. It has to be seen to be believed. Vantage+Wrath is one of the easiest and most powerful builds to create in Engage, especially if you set it up in advance. If you're playing Maddening Difficulty for the 1st time, do not hesitate to use this build as it will save you a lot of headache and frustration. <br><br>
	
Despite all this power, this build is not perfect. The main weakness is <b>Ranged Foes</b> who can't be countered with a Killer Axe


</details>

<details>
<summary> Invincible Tank</summary>

This build revolves around maximizing Ike's bulk and tankiness. I recommend putting Ike on a <b>General or Great Knight (who can use Axes)</b>. These classes have great Strength stats which combined with the high might of Ike's Engage Weapons, leads to more consistent 1-Hit Kills and stronger Great Aethers. Furthermore, these classes don't care about their Speed stat or dodging so the Avoid penalty of Laguz Friend is negligible to them. <br><br>

The main weakness with this build is Mages as Generals and to a lesser extent, Great Knights have bad Resistance Stats which makes them susceptible to magic. You also need to worry about Chain Attacks as the enemy loves to sprinkle Backup Units among their ranks. However, this weakness can be patched up with a certain Sync Skill from another Emblem.


</details>




# **Final Thoughts** <br>

Out of all the Emblems in the game, Ike benefits the most from completing his Paralogue as soon as it unlocks. Doing so takes him from a great Emblem to a fantastic Emblem. He can turn anyone into a nigh invincible tank of destruction with his kit but his main weakness is still Chain Attacks which can be a pain. If you want to push Ike to his limits with the Vantage+Wrath Combo, it is definitely a good idea to pick out your Main Ike User well in advance and have them inherit Vantage from Leif in preparation for Ike. Do this and watch enemies melt before anyone foolish enough to attack Ike’s Main User.
